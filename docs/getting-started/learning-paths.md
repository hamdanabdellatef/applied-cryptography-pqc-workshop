# Choose a learning path

Sessions 1–16, the architecture capstone, review worksheets, and Labs 1 and 4–6 are developed. Labs 2–3 remain scaffolds.

Continue with the [Day 3 study path](../day-3/index.md) for key management, secure architecture and migration. Each lesson links to a separate instructor guide and notebook. Finish with the [architecture capstone](../capstone/index.md), which includes a worksheet, incident scenarios, grading rubric and worked design.

## Study independently

1. Start with [security requirements and threat models](../day-1/01-security-requirements.md). Complete the [worksheet](../resources/threat-model-worksheet.md), including the scenario involving highly confidential data and a state actor.
2. Read the [AEAD self-study lesson](../day-1/02-symmetric-aead.md). Predict the outcomes and use the expandable answers to check your reasoning.
3. Follow the [Lab 1 manual](../labs/lab-01-aead.md) and run the downloadable notebook. The notebook includes explanations, experiments, hints, checks, and a reference implementation.
4. Compare your work with the [Lab 1 walkthrough](../labs/lab-01-aead-solutions.md), then complete the exit questions without looking.
5. Continue with [Session 3](../day-1/03-hashes-passwords-kdfs.md) and its downloadable notebook. Allow 90–120 minutes to study hashes, HMAC, password verification, and key derivation and complete the experiments.
6. Study [Session 4](../day-1/04-classical-public-key.md) and run its notebook. Allow 90–120 minutes for key agreement, authentication failures, signatures, and forward secrecy. Lab 2 remains forthcoming.
7. Study [Session 5](../day-1/05-digital-signatures.md) and its notebook. Allow 75–100 minutes for digital signatures, trusted keys, signed manifests, and policy rejection. Labs 2–3 remain forthcoming.

Allow 60–90 minutes for Session 1 and its worksheet, 75–100 minutes for the AEAD lesson, and 90–120 minutes for Lab 1 on a first independent attempt. Take breaks between units.

## Teach a group

For Day 2, follow the [day overview](../day-2/index.md) and the linked instructor guides for Sessions 6–11. Use the Lab 4–6 facilitation pages for predictions, staged hints and debriefs. Their notebooks run independently of the unfinished Day 1 labs.

1. Use the [Session 1 instructor page](../teach/01-security-requirements.md) for the 40-minute requirements and threat-model discussion.
2. Continue with the [AEAD instructor page](../teach/02-aead.md), which includes explanations to give, diagrams to discuss, timing, and misconception checks.
3. Use the [instructor lab guide](../teach/lab-01-aead.md) for the 60-minute guided lab.
4. Refer learners to the self-study lessons before and after class. The longer extensions can be assigned for independent work.
5. Use the [Session 3 instructor guide](../teach/03-hashes-passwords-kdfs.md) for the next 60-minute session and its prediction questions, demonstrations, and exit checks.
6. Continue with the [Session 4 instructor guide](../teach/04-classical-public-key.md) for a 60-minute explanation of public-key roles, the simulated substitution attack, and forward secrecy.
7. Use the [Session 5 instructor guide](../teach/05-digital-signatures.md) for 45 minutes on signatures, exact bytes, publisher trust, and release policy.

Instructor pages and solutions are intentionally public teaching resources. They are not a place for private assessments or participant information.
